# What we say: messaging playbook

Version 1, 2026-11-13. Everything here is a starting hypothesis. The first batch is ten companies, far too few to prove anything; it is read for what the replies say. Each message is tagged with the choices below so later batches can compare them.

## Who we are writing to

The owner of a local home-services company, roughly $400K to $3M in revenue, who is paying for Google ads right now and has a gap in measuring them. They are busy, they get pitched by marketers constantly, and most pitches are generic.

## The idea

Tell them one true, specific thing about their own advertising that they can check, in their language, and offer to show the evidence. The research does the selling; the message only has to prove we did it.

## Rules

1. **One finding, stated plainly.** "Your ads are running but nothing on the site tracks phone calls," not "you lack call attribution."
2. **Only what was found.** A message may state only what the audit or research recorded. Counts are "about"; absences are "I couldn't find", never "you don't have".
3. **Say what we cannot see.** Every absence comes with the honest hedge ("it may be measured somewhere I can't see"), which doubles as an easy question to answer.
4. **Show how we know.** Name the source: Google's public ad library, their own website.
5. **Ask a question they can answer in one line.** "Are calls being tracked some other way?" or "Do you know whether whoever set up the ads is tracking leads?"
6. **A small ask.** Offer to send what we found. The first message never asks for a meeting.
7. **No pitch in the first message.** Proof and the offer come in the follow-up.
8. **Every touch adds something new.** No "just bumping this".
9. **Short and plain.** First email under about 150 words, social notes under about 70, plain text, no links in the first message, no images.
10. **A way out.** Every email carries the business address and a one-line opt-out. An opt-out on any channel stops everything.

## First email

| Part | What it does |
|---|---|
| Subject | Short and specific to them: "calls from your Google ads", "your new Google ads" |
| Who and why | One sentence: a small Denver ads shop that was looking at companies like theirs |
| The finding | What is running (from the ad library) and the one thing we could not find |
| Why it matters | One sentence, in terms of jobs and phone calls, not metrics |
| Hedge and question | What we cannot see from outside, asked as a question |
| Offer | "I can send over what I found: a few screenshots, nothing to sign up for." |

## Findings and how they are worded

| Finding | When it applies | Plain wording |
|---|---|---|
| No call tracking | Ads running, ad tag present, phone number shown, no call tracking found | "When someone clicks an ad and calls instead of filling in the form, that lead never gets tied back to the ad." |
| No conversion tag | Ads running, analytics present, no Google Ads tag on the site or in Tag Manager | "Google can't tell which clicks turned into estimate requests or calls, so it has nothing to learn from." |
| No tracking at all | Ads running, no analytics or ad tracking found | "There's no way to see which of that ad spend produced a call, and Google has never been told either." |

A finding is used only when the ad check confirms the company's own ads are running. Ads run by a third-party programme such as the Better Business Bureau do not count.

## The sequence

| Day | Channel | Purpose |
|---|---|---|
| 0 | Email | The finding, a question, the offer |
| 2 | Facebook page message | Two or three sentences: is this the right person, the finding, who should it go to |
| 5 | Email | Proof from a similar company, then their gap restated |
| 10 | Email | A polite close with a two-word way to say yes ("send it") |

- No email address: Facebook only, with one follow-up on day 6.
- No social page: the three emails only.
- LinkedIn: only when the owner's own profile is found. None was in the first batch.
- A reply on any channel stops the rest.

## Proof

One case, described the way LaunchTec's own site describes it: a home-exteriors company had run Google ads for years without tracing a sale to them; a big part of the problem was broken tracking; after the tracking and campaigns were rebuilt they closed $240K in three months. It appears in the follow-up, never the opener.

## What gets recorded

Every message carries: the angle (finding-first), the finding type, the call to action, where the proof appears, and the segment. The original draft is kept beside the sent version, so edits can be studied. Replies are logged as interested, not now, not interested, wrong person, or asked not to be contacted.

## What to test next, once there is volume

In rough order of expected effect:

1. Finding type: which gap gets the most replies.
2. Channel: email, Facebook page, both.
3. Proof in the opener, or held for the follow-up.
4. The ask: a question plus an offer, or an offer of a short call.
5. Who it is addressed to: the named owner, or "whoever looks after the advertising".

Telling a 2% reply rate from 4% takes about 1,100 companies per version, so only the first one or two are testable soon. The rest stay as recorded attributes until the numbers are there.
